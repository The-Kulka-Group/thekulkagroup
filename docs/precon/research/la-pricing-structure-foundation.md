# Pricing for construction type and foundation add-ons

Researched 2026-09-28 for the Design and Engineering layer. This file suggests budget rates for
the cost items that `la-construction-type.md` and `la-foundation-system.md` turn on: a concrete
podium deck and its waterproofing, fire-retardant-treated (FRT) exterior wall framing, extra rated
gypsum layers, deep foundations, ground improvement, underpinning and rock excavation. Every
figure is a suggestion for Matt to accept, change or reject. None of them is a Kulka rate until he
states it. Kulka AI states planning assumptions only. It does not read drawings or perform
takeoffs.

Published cost data for this scope is thin. RSMeans line items sit behind a paid licence, and the
free pages that quote per-square-foot costs are mostly contractor blogs. The firmest numbers we
found are Kulka's own estimate files, public DOT bid prices (New York 2021, Michigan 2024 to 2025)
and the FHWA ground modification manual. Where a figure rests on our own arithmetic, the text says
so.

All figures are 2026 dollars, open shop, New York suburbs (Long Island, Westchester, Rockland,

## 13a. Type IA concrete podium deck

**Suggested: $34 per SF of podium deck. Range $25 to $45.**

**Area.** The plan area of the elevated concrete deck that carries the wood building, usually the
podium footprint. It is not gross square feet. `canonical-work-items.md` item 3 uses GSF as its
driver today; this rate needs deck area, which is roughly the footprint when the podium covers the
site.

**What is included.** Shoring and reshoring, formwork, mild rebar or post-tensioning (strand,
anchors, stressing), 5,000 psi concrete, pumping, placing, finishing and curing, and the thickened
areas and beams that pick up wood bearing walls off the column grid, up to about a 12 inch average
thickness. A 10 to 12 inch slab also meets the 3-hour rating Type IA asks of the floor.

**What is not included.** Podium columns, shear walls and stair or elevator cores below the deck
(see below), the slab at grade under the podium, waterproofing, fireproofing of any steel, and the
gypcrete above (item 3a).

**Basis.**

1. **Kulka's own number.** The Vue, 426 A Main, conceptual budget carries "L2 Podium, 12" avg
   assumed" at $1,395,525 lump sum. The same file carries a "Slab Vapor Barrier Upgrade" of
   40,894 SF, which we read as the footprint. If the deck matches that area, the podium priced at
   **$34.13 per SF**, or about $921 per CY at 12 inches. The file does not state deck area or date,
   so both readings are ours. The companion Vue file is dated June 2023. Escalated from 2023 to
   2025 it would be $36 to $39.
2. **Terrapin Construction Group, Denver (April 2026).** Post-tensioned elevated decks $14 to $24
   per SF nationally, $19 to $26 in the Northeast ("Highest PT installed cost in U.S."). Mild
   two-way flat plate $12 to $20 nationally. Its worked 8 inch PT example totals $17.20 per SF with
   formwork at only $3.80. We read these as slab-only figures for thinner slabs than a transfer
   podium, and they sit below Kulka's own number.
3. **Terrapin, multifamily cost per unit (April 2026).** A whole Type I podium for a 4 or 5 over 1
   runs $35 to $70 per SF of podium footprint, but that figure includes foundations, columns,
   transfer beams, plumbing and sprinkler risers and electrical distribution. Only the deck part
   compares to the $34 above.
4. **Our build-up, 11 inch average slab.** Concrete 0.034 CY per SF at about $190 per CY delivered
   (Northeast ready-mix $175 to $220 per CY in 2026 per public price surveys) plus waste, about $7.
   Placing, pumping, finishing and curing $2.50 to $3.50. Formwork with shoring and reshoring at a
   12 ft podium story, $10 to $14. Rebar at about 5.5 psf and $1.60 per pound, or PT strand plus
   lighter mild steel, $8 to $9. Total **$28 to $34**. The unit rates in this build-up are our
   assumptions for open-shop downstate work, not sourced figures.

PT against conventional does not change the budget number much. Terrapin puts PT $1.50 to $4 per
SF above flat plate on the same deck, but PT usually allows a thinner slab, which offsets it. The
rule does not need to pick one.

**Columns and walls below the deck.** Not in the deck rate. Our arithmetic: 18 to 24 inch columns
at 25 to 30 ft bays and 12 ft height, plus concrete shear walls or cores, add about **$7 per SF of
deck, range $4 to $12**. No source gave this split; treat it as a placeholder until a Kulka podium
job shows it.

**Location and escalation.** Kulka's figure is local and needs only escalation. Terrapin's
Northeast range is already regional.

**Where it prices.** Item 3, Elevated Slabs and Podium Deck, per SF of deck. Columns and walls
under it belong in item 3 as well, or in a separate container, so the deck rate stays comparable.

### Podium waterproofing

**Suggested: $14 per SF of exposed podium. Range $9 to $22.**

**When it is typical.** A podium deck needs a waterproofing membrane where it is exposed to
weather above occupied or parking space: courtyards, amenity terraces, plazas and sidewalks built
over the garage. Under the footprint of the wood building, the deck is an interior floor and does
not carry a podium membrane. So the quantity is the exposed podium area, not the whole deck. We
found no source for how much of a typical deck is exposed; it depends on the site plan.

**What is included.** Hot fluid-applied rubberized asphalt (about 215 mil, reinforced) or a
comparable sheet system, primer, flashings at walls, drains and penetrations, protection board and
a drainage composite.

**What is not included.** Rigid insulation, pavers or pedestals, soil and planters, drains
themselves, and a vehicular traffic coating on any exposed parking level (item 3's separate
container).

**Basis.** This is the weakest figure in the file.

- Product literature (Siplast PA-750, Tremco, W. R. Meadows HRM 714) confirms the system and its
  parts but gives no prices.
- Consumer and supplier pages put hot rubberized asphalt at $2 to $5 per SF for the membrane alone
  and sheet or liquid membranes at $2 to $6.
- A Vancouver contractor (WCP Building Renewal, July 2025) quotes $25 to $40 per SF for podium deck
  membrane replacement, which includes tear-off and overburden removal, in Canadian dollars.
- Our build-up for new work: membrane $4 to $7, protection board and drainage composite $2 to $4,
  flashings, detailing and access $3 to $8, giving $9 to $19 before a New York labor premium.

**Where it prices.** Item 3, Podium Waterproofing Membrane container, per SF of exposed podium.

## 13b. Fire-retardant-treated lumber in Type III exterior walls

**Suggested: $1.75 per SF of gross exterior wall. Range $1.10 to $3.00. Spread over the building,
about $0.90 per GSF, range $0.55 to $1.60.**

**Area.** Gross exterior wall area of the framed floors, before deducting openings (headers and
jambs use extra lumber, so openings do not save much). To convert to GSF, Kulka's The Vue,
426 W Main (June 2023) priced 53,038.67 SF of exterior wall framing labor on a building of
101,200 GSF, a ratio of **0.52**. Multiply the wall rate by that ratio, or by the job's own ratio
when the building shape is known.

**What is included.** The premium of FRT over untreated material for studs, plates, blocking and
headers in exterior walls, and for FRT plywood over untreated sheathing, plus about 15 percent for
handling, waste and extra studs where the engineer applies the treatment's strength reduction
(STRUCTURE magazine notes FRT lumber and sheathing carry manufacturer-specific strength reduction
factors). Framing labor does not change otherwise.

**Basis.**

1. **Lumber premium, same seller.** Dunn Lumber, read September 2026: Douglas fir 2x6 #2 & Better
   $1.17 to $1.21 per LF; Dricon fire-retardant Douglas fir 2x6 #2 & Better from $1.88 per LF. The
   premium is about $0.70 per LF, or $0.70 per board foot on a 2x6, near 60 percent. These are
   retail prices in Seattle; contractor pricing here will differ, so we carry $0.45 to $0.75 per
   board foot.
2. **Sheathing premium, same seller.** LENCO Supplies, Buffalo NY: 1/2" x 4 x 8 fire-retardant
   plywood $41.95, 1/2" CDX $26.49. The premium is $15.46 per sheet, $0.48 per SF, again near 60
   percent. With waste, about $0.55 per SF of wall; we carry $0.45 to $1.20.
3. **Our quantity arithmetic.** A 2x6 exterior bearing wall at 16 inches on center with plates,
   blocking and headers uses about 1.3 to 2.0 board feet per SF of wall; lower floors of a
   five-story building sit at the high end. At 1.5 BF and $0.60 per BF, lumber adds about $0.90.
   Lumber $0.90 plus sheathing $0.55 is $1.45, plus 15 percent is about $1.70.

**Check against Kulka's files.** The Vue files already price "4x8x1/2" LP Flame Block Sheathing,"
58,342.54 SF at $4.13 (426 W Main, June 2023) and at $2.44 (revised conceptual). LP FlameBlock is
a fire-rated OSB with a magnesium oxide cement face, which LP says can be used in Type II, III and
V construction and carries the same design values as untreated panels. It is not FRT plywood. If a
job carries FlameBlock or FRT sheathing already, take the sheathing part out and use about **$1.20
per SF of exterior wall** for the lumber alone.

**Check against ICC.** ICC's August 2026 valuation data puts R-2 Type IIIA at $190.52 per SF and
Type VA at $167.24, a $23 gap (see `la-construction-type.md`). FRT and the extra gypsum below
explain only $2 to $4 of that. ICC says its table is not an estimating guide, so the gap should
not be read as the cost of Type III.

**Location and escalation.** The two price checks are current retail. No factor applied.

**Where it prices.** Item 8, Wood Framing, as an FRT premium container, turned on by Type III.

## 13c. Added layers of rated gypsum board

**Suggested: $1.60 per SF of wall, per added layer of 5/8" Type X, per face. Range $1.00 to $2.50.
For an added layer of Type X exterior gypsum sheathing, $2.25, range $1.60 to $3.25 (our
estimate).**

**What changes by type.** From `la-construction-type.md`, Table 601:

- **Type VA to Type IIIA or IIIB.** Exterior bearing walls go from 1 hour to 2 hours. Interior
  bearing walls and floors stay at 1 hour in IIIA. So the change sits on exterior walls only.
- **A 2-hour exterior wall.** It must be rated from inside when more than 10 ft from the lot line,
  and from both sides at 10 ft or less (IBC 705.5, as WoodWorks' 2026 Type III slides quote it).
  The usual step is one more layer of 5/8" Type X on the inside face. Where the wall must be rated
  from outside too, it also takes a layer of Type X exterior sheathing, or a tested assembly that
  uses FRT or FlameBlock sheathing in place of it. Which layers a job needs depends on the tested
  assembly the architect picks.
- **Type VB to Type VA.** Floors, bearing walls and roof go to 1 hour. Floors between units are
  1-hour anyway (IBC 711.2.4.3), so the extra cost is mostly bearing walls and the roof.
- **Any wall from 1 hour to 2 hours.** Typically one added layer per rated face.

**Per SF of exterior wall, Type V to Type III:** $1.60 with the inside layer only, $3.85 with
inside and outside layers. Spread over GSF at the 0.52 ratio: $0.85 to $2.00.

**What is included.** Board, screws, waste and hanging an unfinished base layer. The finished face
layer is already in the base rate. Fire caulk at the wall and floor intersections and extra
blocking that WoodWorks shows at Type III floor lines are not included.

**Basis.**

1. **Kulka's files.** The Vue, 426 W Main and 426 A Main price "1/2" GWB Walls" at $2.00 per SF.
   Jefferson's Ferry prices "5/8" GWB Ceilings & Soffits" at $6.00 per SF. A hung, unfinished
   inner layer should cost less than Kulka's finished-wall rate.
2. **Terrapin, commercial drywall (May 2026).** 5/8" Type X hung and finished to Level 4 runs $3.20
   to $4.80 per SF nationally and $4.20 to $8.20 in the Northeast's large cities (union). Level 4
   finishing alone is $2.20 to $3.20 of that. Two-hour partitions with a double layer of Type X on
   each side add $1.20 to $2.00 per SF of wall over single-layer work, nationally.
3. **Material.** Retail 5/8" Type X runs about $16 to $22 per 4 x 8 sheet (HomeGuide, 2026),
   $0.50 to $0.70 per SF.

We did not find a price for Type X glass-mat exterior sheathing; the $2.25 assumes material near
twice interior Type X.

**Location and escalation.** Terrapin's national figures times 1.10. Kulka's figure is local.

**Where it prices.** Item 17, Metal Framing and Drywall, as a rated-layer container keyed to
exterior wall area and the construction type.

| Auger cast (CFA) pile, 16 inch | $60 per LF | $40 to $90 | Our estimate; old FHWA data as a floor |
| Helical pile, about 50 kips allowable, 25 to 35 ft | $3,000 each | $1,800 to $5,500 | Michigan DOT 2025; Helical Pile World 2018 |
| Rammed aggregate pier, 24 to 30 inch, 12 to 15 ft | $1,200 each (about $80 per LF) | $800 to $2,000 | ASPE paper 2015; FHWA GEC 13 |
| Vibro stone column | Same band as aggregate piers | | FHWA GEC 13 |
| Dynamic compaction | $4 per SF of treated area | $2 to $7 | FHWA GEC 13 |
| Mobilization, driven piles | $40,000 | $25,000 to $75,000 | Our estimate; Michigan DOT |

| Mobilization and load test, aggregate piers | $40,000 | $25,000 to $60,000 | Our estimate |

**Driven piles.** Michigan DOT's 2024 to 2025 weighted average report prices "Pile, Steel, Furn and
Driven, 12 inch" at $32 to $92 per ft (most bids $32 to $42) and 14 inch at $45 to $81 (most near
$55 to $80). Cast-in-place concrete 14 inch runs $21 to $65 per ft, 16 inch $55. "Pile Driving
Equipment, Furn" lump sums run $25,000 to $200,000 on most contracts, with a few far higher on
large bridges. Dynamic pile tests run $1,500 to $3,600 each. Times about 1.2 for location and
1.07 to reach 2026, 12 inch steel lands near $50 to $55 and 14 inch near $70 to $80. New York's own
2021 prices for steel H-piles (item 551.01) came in at $10 to $125 per meter, below the cost of the
steel, which reads as unbalanced bidding or split pay items. We did not use them. On Long Island,
timber piles are a common cheaper choice for light buildings; we found no current price for them.

**Auger cast piles.** FHWA's GeoTechTools cost page (via the Geo-Institute) lists CFA piles 12 to
24 inch at $10 to $40 per LF with $20,000 to $40,000 mobilization, from 2003 to 2011 DOT bids at
quantities over 2,000 LF. That is too old and too large-quantity to escalate with any confidence,
so it serves only as a floor. FHWA GEC 8 says CFA piles are used more in private commercial work
than highway work because they are fast and quiet. Our $60 assumes 16 inch piles, 35 to 70 ft,
downstate, and costs close to driven steel per foot. Spoils from auger cast piles must be hauled,
and on Long Island that can mean testing and paying for contaminated soil, which this rate leaves
out.

**Helical piles.** Michigan DOT, first quarter 2025: "Helical Pile, Furn" $840 each for 50 piles,
plus "Helical Pile, Installed" $36.14 per ft for 1,232 ft, about $1,700 per 25 ft pile, and $3,000
per test pile. Helical Pile World's October 2018 survey put a Northeast new-construction pile
(20 ft, 20 kips) at $840, which escalates to about $1,200. Building piles carrying 40 to 100 kips
run deeper and on larger shafts, hence $3,000. The CHANCE manual calls helicals suited to
"relatively light to medium loads," and our per-SF arithmetic agrees: they are dear under a podium.

**Aggregate piers and stone columns.** FHWA GEC 13 (Table 1-6, November 2016) lists stone columns
and rammed aggregate piers at $15 to $60 per lineal foot, deep dynamic compaction at $10 to $30 per
square yard, and vibro-compaction at $5 to $9 per lineal foot, with mobilization "from a few hundred
dollars to more than $100,000." Escalated by 1.57 and located by 1.10, aggregate columns run $26 to
$104 per LF and dynamic compaction $1.90 to $5.80 per SF. An ASPE technical paper (2015) prices a
92-pier job of 24 inch, 10 to 15 ft piers at $78,772 including overhead and profit, about $856 per
pier and $78 per LF; escalated and located, about $1,550 per pier. Our $1,200 sits between them.
Geopier says its piers cost "30 to 50 percent less than deep foundations"; that is the maker's
claim.

**Dynamic compaction.** Drops a heavy weight across open ground. The vibration rules it out next to
existing buildings, which covers most infill sites in Kulka's area, so we suggest it only for open
sites with deep loose fill. The treated area runs past the footprint by roughly the depth treated;
that margin is our reading.

### Per SF of building footprint

This is our arithmetic. Average pressure per SF of footprint comes from `la-foundation-system.md`
(dead plus unreduced live load on every level the foundation carries). We multiply by 1.3 because
caps need at least two or three piles and piles cluster under walls and columns, then divide by
an assumed allowable capacity per pile: 140 kips driven steel, 100 kips auger cast, 50 kips helical,
60 kips per aggregate pier. Pile length is assumed at 50 ft driven, 45 ft auger cast, 30 ft helical.
Mobilization is spread over a 20,000 SF footprint.

| Building | Pressure | Driven steel | Auger cast | Helical | Aggregate piers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood, 3 stories on grade | 220 psf | $9 (2 piles per 1,000 SF) | $9 | $18 | $8 |
| Wood, 4 stories on grade | 303 psf | $12 (2.8) | $12 | $24 | $10 |
| Wood, 5 stories on grade | 385 psf | $15 (3.6) | $15 | $31 | $12 |
| Wood 4 over 1 | 562 psf | $20 (5.2) | $21 | $44 | $17 |
| Wood 5 over 1 | 644 psf | $23 (6.0) | $24 | $51 | $19 |
| Wood 5 over 2 | 903 psf | $31 (8.4) | $33 | $71 | $25 |
| Light gauge, 7 stories on grade | up to 778 psf | $27 (7.2) | $29 | $61 | $22 |

Dollars are per SF of footprint. Changing the pile length from 50 ft to 30 or 80 ft moves the
driven and auger cast figures by about 40 percent either way, which is most of the range in the
short answer. That is why we give ranges near half to twice the suggested figure.

**Which system for which building (our reading).** Driven or auger cast piles for podium buildings
on poor ground and for any building over peat, marsh or thick lake clay. Helical piles for light
buildings on tight or low-access sites. Aggregate piers for light buildings on loose sand, fill or
medium clay, which `la-foundation-system.md` names as the ground improvement case. For a podium on
soft ground, rigid inclusions (grouted piers) replace aggregate piers; we found no price for them.

**What the pile rates do not include.** Pile caps, grade beams and the change from slab on grade to
a structural slab (item 2), obstructions and predrilling, spoils testing and disposal, dewatering,
static load tests, and the geotechnical engineer's inspection. These are real and can match the
pile cost itself; we did not price them.

**Kulka's data point.** West Shore Road's "Piles Allowance" of $350,000 (code 31.06010 Pile Caps)
over 16,357 SF of "Excavate Foundation" is $21.40 per SF of footprint. The file does not say the
pile type, count, length or whether caps are in it. It fits a podium building or a heavy light
building on driven or auger cast piles. It is one job, and the suggestions above do not depend
on it.

**Location and escalation.** Michigan prices times about 1.2 for location and 1.07 for 2024 to
2026. FHWA 2016 prices times 1.57 and 1.10. ASPE 2015 prices times 1.65 and 1.10.

**Where it prices.** Item 34a, Deep Foundations, Piles and Shoring, as a flagged allowance per SF
of footprint, with a note of the pile type assumed. Ground improvement goes in item 34a or a new
line, per `la-foundation-system.md`.

## 15. Underpinning an adjacent building

**Suggested: $1,100 per LF of underpinned wall, for pit underpinning about 8 ft below the existing
footing. Range $700 to $2,000. Add $25,000 for a preconstruction survey, movement monitoring and
the engineer's design, range $15,000 to $50,000.**

**Typical depth.** The case that matters is one basement or garage level dug beside a building on
shallow footings, which puts the new bottom 8 to 12 ft below the old footing. Our arithmetic for a
deeper cut: about $125 to $175 more per LF for each added foot, until micropiles become cheaper.

**What is included.** Hand-dug pits in sequence, concrete, dry-pack to the old footing, and the
excavation and disposal from the pits.

**Basis.**

- A New York City engineering firm's guide (Asvakas Engineering) puts traditional pit underpinning
  at $800 to $1,500 per LF of foundation, micropile underpinning at $1,500 to $3,500 or more per
  pile, and $8,000 to $25,000 for engineering, drawings and filing. We read these figures in the
  search result; the page itself would not open (a certificate error), so treat them as
  secondhand.
- UK commercial underpinning runs £1,500 to £3,500 per linear metre (2024), about $580 to $1,350
  per LF at current exchange rates. Ontario basement underpinning runs $500 to $800 per lineal foot
  (Canadian, residential).
- Our build-up: a pit about 3.5 ft wide and 8 ft deep holds about 1 CY per LF; at $1,000 to $1,600
  per CY in place for hand work, $1,000 to $1,600 per LF.
- Micropiles as the alternative: Michigan DOT 2024 "Micropile, Type A, Furn and Install" at $5,900
  to $8,800 each; New York 2021 micropiles at $260 to $330 per LF (Region 11 and Region 5). At 4 to
  6 ft spacing plus needle beams, our arithmetic gives $1,500 to $3,000 per LF.

**Location and escalation.** The New York City figures are metro prices and likely high for
Rockland or Suffolk open shop, which is why the suggestion sits near the middle of their range.

**Where it prices.** Item 34a, as a new flagged Underpinning container, per LF of adjacent wall.
`la-foundation-system.md` step 7 already raises the flag: likely when the work goes below grade
beside an existing building, possible otherwise. Support of excavation stays its own line
(Kulka's West Shore file carries a $375,000 allowance for it; the 426 A Main Vue file carries a
$200,000 shoring allowance).

## 15. Rock excavation in the Hudson Valley and Westchester

**Suggested, per CY measured in place (bank), including loading, hauling and off-site disposal or
on-site crushing within about 15 miles:**

| Method | Suggested | Range | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ripping, mass | $45 | $30 to $70 | Weathered or fractured rock a large excavator or dozer can rip |
| Hoe ram, mass | $100 | $65 to $150 | Sound rock where blasting is not allowed |
| Controlled blasting, mass | $85 | $60 to $140 | Sound rock, larger volumes, a town that permits blasting |
| Trench, footing and pit rock | $225 | $125 to $450 | Small volumes: footings, elevator pits, utility trenches |

**What is included.** Breaking, loading and disposal. For blasting, the per-CY figure includes
drilling, blasting mats and seismographs; a preconstruction survey of neighbors and the permit add
a lump sum we estimate at $15,000 to $40,000. Rock swells to 1.5 to 1.65 times its bank volume
when broken, so trucking and tipping add roughly $25 to $45 per bank CY inside these figures. The
swell and disposal numbers are our assumptions.

**Basis.**

- **Backwell Excavation, central New York (April 2026).** Hydraulic hammer $35 to $50 per CY for
  soft weathered rock, $45 to $65 for standard access, $60 to $90 or more for hard rock with poor
  access. Blasting $55 to $75 simple, $75 to $90 or more near structures, $90 to $150 in urban
  conditions with vibration limits. Syracuse sits at 0.99 against White Plains 1.20, so downstate
  prices run about 20 percent higher. The article does not say whether disposal is included.
- **New York State DOT, 2021.** "Unclassified Excavation and Disposal" in Region 8 (Westchester,
  Rockland, Putnam, Orange, Dutchess, Ulster, Columbia) averaged $41.01 per CY on 21,056 CY and
  $43.54 on 8,887 CY; small quantities ran $188 to $241. Region 10 (Long Island) averaged $41.56 on
  89,810 CY. That item mixes earth and rock. Escalated by 1.29, the large-quantity prices are about
  $53 to $56 per CY. The same year, Region 7 bid "Excavation and Disposal of Rock" at $40 per CY for
  131 CY and $250 for 10 CY.
- **Michigan DOT, 2024.** "Excavation, Rock Fdn" $40 to $134 per CY on 181 to 227 CY. "Excavation,
  Rock" $50 to $1,200 per CY on 3 to 50 CY.
- **City of Yonkers bids.** Wilson Park, February 2024: rock excavation, 5 CY, bid at $1, $1, $20,
  $300, $500, $650, $650 and $2,000 per CY by eight bidders. St. Denis demolition, September 2021:
  rock removal per 10 CY increment at $10,000, $950 and $10,000. Small unit-price items like these
  carry bid loading, but they show small rock quantities in Westchester price in the hundreds of
  dollars per CY.

**Location and escalation.** Backwell times about 1.2. DOT 2021 times 1.29.

**Where it prices.** Item 34, Rock Excavation container, per bank CY, in the ZIPs
`la-foundation-system.md` flags for rock (prefixes 105 to 109 and 124 to 126). Mass rock sits with
bulk excavation for a basement or garage; trench and pit rock sits with footings, elevator pits and
utility trenches. Before borings, it should be a TBD line outside the total, as the foundation rule
treats every unknown.

## What we could not find

- RSMeans line items for flat plate, PT slabs, FRT lumber, gypsum layers, piles, underpinning or
  rock. They sit behind a paid licence whose terms, per `cost-index-research.md`, do not yet allow
  use in Kulka AI.
- A published podium deck cost from Cumming, RLB, Gordian or WoodWorks. RLB and Cumming publish
  whole-building costs by city, not component rates.
- A price for new podium waterproofing in New York. The file's $14 rests on a build-up.
- A published FRT premium from WoodWorks, APA or a treater. WoodWorks' Type III material covers
  code and detailing, not cost. Our FRT figure rests on two retail price comparisons.
- A price for Type X glass-mat exterior sheathing.
- Current New York DOT bid prices. The 2021 file on the NYSDOT repository is the latest public
  download we found. The newer bid-history tool loads its data through an API that uses a login
  embedded in the page; we did not use it. The Thruway's 2024 tabulations returned web pages
  instead of files.
- Deep Foundations Institute, ADSC and Helical Pile Association cost material. None is public.
- Any published price for auger cast piles newer than 2011, for timber piles on Long Island, or
  for rigid inclusions.
- A published open-shop location factor for these counties. The 1.10 is our judgment.
- The deck area, pile type, pile count and dates behind Kulka's own figures (426 A Main podium,
  West Shore piles). Both would sharpen these suggestions if Matt can fill them in.
- Blasting rules by town. Several Westchester and Rockland municipalities regulate or restrict
  blasting; we did not check each one.
