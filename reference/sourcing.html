How we adopt

The owner retired the old rebuild-everything rule (2026-08-25). The working stance:

Library defaults are welcome. A well-made component ships as it comes. We tune it to Daylight where the change is cheap and skip vanity rebuilds.
One palette. Daylight's colors are the product's colors, so vendor focus rings, selection blues, and status rainbows get mapped to daylight.css tokens.
Buy behavior. Focus handling, keyboard support, ARIA, and positioning come from the library. We never rebuild solved problems.
Motion stays quiet. Quick, interruptible, and respectful of reduced motion, matching the shipped 150/200/250ms tokens.

Element by element

Each card names what the CRM needs, the pick, the alternative, and the interaction references worth studying before we build.

Primitive layer

Base UI

Popovers, dialogs, menus, selects, tooltips, focus management. The invisible machinery under everything else.

Why
Base UI shipped stable 1.0 in December 2025, is staffed full time by MUI, and several engineers who built Radix now work on it. Radix slowed after the WorkOS acquisition. shadcn/ui now targets either library with a one-line switch, so we keep the shadcn component structure and point it at Base UI.
Runner-up
Radix UI. Still solid, still everywhere; the risk is maintenance pace, not quality.
Steal
The shadcn component anatomy and file layout; our components stay copy-owned source, not a dependency.
Refuse
The shadcn zinc palette; it swaps for Daylight tokens. The structural defaults (radii, spacing, focus rings) stay unless they clash on sight.

The board

TanStack Table + TanStack Virtual

The P-101 pursuits board: grouped rows, the apparatus margin, status chips, inline editing, a sticky header, and the load strip. The flagship surface.

Why
Headless and MIT. It handles the table logic (grouping, sorting, selection, column state) and renders nothing, which is the point: the sheet, the margin, and the group bands are our markup. Virtualization comes from the same family when boards grow past a few hundred rows.
Runner-up
react-data-grid, if a future surface ever needs true spreadsheet behavior across fifty-plus columns. AG Grid is refused outright: its chrome, theming system, and enterprise licensing all fight the sheet.
Steal
Monday's cell interaction: click a cell, edit in place, change status from the chip itself. Attio's record-table editing has the best feel shipping today. Linear's keyboard row navigation (j/k, enter to open).
Refuse
Toolbar farms, filter chips above the table, column menus with twenty options. The board is a drafted sheet, not a data IDE.

Inline cell editing

Custom cells on Base UI popovers

Status changes from the chip, dates from a small calendar, owners from a searchable list, text in place. Editing never leaves the row.

Why
No library owns this pattern well; it is composition. TanStack cell renderers plus Base UI popover and select primitives, with optimistic state so the cell updates on pointer down.
Steal
Attio for the popover feel and keyboard flow. Notion for enter-to-commit, esc-to-revert semantics that users already know.
Refuse
Modal editing of any kind. A dialog to change a status is a defect.

Command search

cmdk

The rail search becomes the product's front door: type "P-101" and go, type a client name and land on the record, type "new pursuit" and act.

Why
Battle-tested, tiny, accessible, and the engine behind the palettes users already love. Spoken sheet numbers make Kulka unusually well suited to a command-first search: every sheet already has a short, sayable address.
Runner-up
modern-cmdk benchmarks faster but is young; hold until it has a year of production miles.
Steal
Linear's cmd-K: recents first, verbs and nouns in one list, no tabs.
Refuse
Full-screen search pages and category tabs inside the palette.

Dates

react-day-picker

Bid due dates on the board, meeting dates on briefs. Short dates ("Sep 04") on boards, ISO in document metadata, per the gauntlet law.

Why
The engine under the shadcn Calendar, small and unstyled enough to take Daylight cleanly. Date logic is a solved problem; we only need it skinned.
Runner-up
React Aria's DatePicker, stronger on keyboard entry of typed dates; adopt it later if estimators ask to type rather than pick.
Refuse
Range-picker flourishes and preset sidebars until a real feature needs them.

Document editor

Tiptap on ProseMirror

The B-series brief today; the S-series scope later. Numbered clause margins, TBD redlines inside text, AI drafts held in pencil until a person accepts, revision deltas. The hardest sourcing call on this page.

Why
Tiptap wraps ProseMirror, whose strict document schema is what our apparatus needs: clauses as typed nodes, margin numbers derived from structure, draft blocks as node attributes with accept and discard as real operations. It is the consensus pick for docs-shaped products, with the largest extension ecosystem. Core is MIT; we build on that and skip the paid cloud tier, since drafts-in-pencil is our own flow, not their comments product.
Runner-up
Lexical (Meta). Better for chat-scale and mobile surfaces, thinner ecosystem for structured documents. ProseMirror raw is the expert path if Tiptap's abstraction ever binds.
Steal
Notion's block hover affordances, kept far quieter. Google Docs suggestion mode as the mental model for AI pencil drafts.
Refuse
Floating formatting bubbles, slash-command menus with forty blocks, and any collaboration presence chrome. A brief has one author and one reviewer.

Forms and validation

react-hook-form + zod

New pursuit, log contact, client profile edits. Small forms, fast, with errors that behave like redlines.

Why
The stable pair: uncontrolled inputs for speed, one schema shared between the form and the API. Error rendering is ours: border turns redline, one 12px sentence under the field says what to fix.
Refuse
Toast-based form errors and summary banners at the top of forms.

Quiet confirmations

sonner

"Saved." "Brief accepted." One sentence, bottom-left, gone in four seconds. DESIGN.md 7.10 already wrote the spec.

Why
The best toast primitive going; we use its timing, stacking, and accessibility and strip the rest to ink on white with a hairline border.
Refuse
Icons, colors, progress rings, celebration variants, and any toast that asks the user to act. Redlines wait in the strip; toasts only confirm.

Drag and reorder

pragmatic-drag-and-drop

Not needed at launch. The board pivots by data, not by dragging cards. Sourced now so a future need does not trigger a rushed pick.

Why
Atlassian's library: framework-agnostic, tiny core, serious accessibility, proven under Trello and Jira load. Better bones than dnd-kit for a product that must stay quiet while dragging.
Refuse
Kanban as a default view. The Session View grid stays the pivot.

Icons

Phosphor (regular)

Rail items only, per the owner's placement pick. Always beside a word, never instead of one.

Why
Owner pick, 2026-08-25, from a rendered comparison against Lucide and Phosphor filled. The rounder shapes sit warmly with Open Sans, and the regular weight keeps the rail from going heavy. Ships as @phosphor-icons/react at 16px.
Runner-up
Lucide, the enterprise-neutral default, if Phosphor's warmth ever reads as soft at smaller sizes.
Refuse
Filled, duotone, and two-color icon styles; emoji in UI; icons in header actions, rows, or content.

Where the craft budget goes

Nothing above is exotic on purpose. Behavior is a commodity now, so we buy it MIT and spend the craft where no library can help: the board's cell-editing feel, the pencil draft flow in the editor, and the command search that understands sheet numbers. Those three interactions are the product's signature moves and they get built, not sourced.

Adoption order matches the build: primitives and forms first, the board second, cmdk third, the editor fourth. Sonner, dates, and icons ride along with whichever surface needs them first.
